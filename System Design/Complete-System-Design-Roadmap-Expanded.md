## Important: What "One Day" Means

Completing a module in one day does **not** mean mastering that module.

The objective of the first pass is to build a **mental map of System Design**.

For every concept, focus on answering:

```text
What is it?
      ↓
Why do we need it?
      ↓
How does it work?
      ↓
When should we use it?
      ↓
What problem does it solve?
      ↓
What are the trade-offs?
```

For example, on the Message Queues day, the goal is not to become a Kafka expert. The goal is to understand concepts such as producers, consumers, queues, partitions, ordering, retries, delivery semantics, and why asynchronous communication is useful.

## Recommended Three-Stage Approach

### Stage 1 — First Pass

Complete Modules 1–19 using the one-day-per-module approach.

**Goal:** Understand the landscape and build a mental model.

```text
Module 1
   ↓
Module 2
   ↓
Module 3
   ↓
...
   ↓
Module 19
```

Do not get stuck trying to achieve complete mastery of one topic before moving forward.

### Stage 2 — Deep Dive

After completing the first pass, revisit the most important System Design building blocks:

```text
Networking
    ↓
Databases
    ↓
Caching
    ↓
Scalability
    ↓
Distributed Systems
    ↓
Message Queues
    ↓
Microservices
    ↓
Cloud Architecture
    ↓
Reliability
    ↓
HLD
```

Spend multiple days on the difficult concepts and understand their internals, trade-offs, and real-world applications.

### Stage 3 — System Design Practice

Start designing real systems and continuously improve the designs as you learn new concepts.

For example:

```text
Learn HTTP
   ↓
Design URL Shortener
   ↓
Learn Database
   ↓
Improve URL Shortener
   ↓
Learn Cache
   ↓
Improve URL Shortener
   ↓
Learn Load Balancing
   ↓
Scale URL Shortener
   ↓
Learn Distributed Systems
   ↓
Make URL Shortener Distributed
```

This approach is more effective than studying every topic theoretically before attempting a design.

## Final Goal

The purpose of the 19-day plan is to move from:

> **"I don't know what concepts exist in System Design."**

to:

> **"I understand the major System Design concepts and know where each one fits."**

Then, through deep dives and repeated system-design exercises, move toward:

> **"I can design a scalable, reliable system and explain the reasoning and trade-offs behind my architectural decisions."**

**Important:** System Design is not about memorizing technologies. The goal is to understand problems, choose appropriate architectural components, justify those choices, and understand the trade-offs involved.
